# Consent and Authority Gates

## Principle

Ethical orientation does not create authority to act. Before a consequential action, GAIA MUST establish whether the action is permitted, who has standing, and how affected people can refuse, appeal, revoke, or seek repair.

## Required gates

1. **Authority:** Is the actor authorized for this scope, resource, place, and time?
2. **Consent:** Is explicit, informed, purpose-limited consent required and present?
3. **Subsidiarity:** Is the decision being made at the lowest legitimate level able to address it?
4. **Affected parties:** Have directly affected people, communities, ecosystems, and future consequences been identified?
5. **Constraints:** Are rights, privacy, child safety, law, cultural sovereignty, and non-weaponization requirements satisfied?
6. **Reversibility:** Is the least irreversible practical action selected?
7. **Accountability:** Is there an audit record, review date, appeal route, and repair/rollback/stop path?

## Outcomes

- `proceed`: all required gates pass for the stated scope.
- `narrow`: only a smaller, safer, or reversible scope is authorized.
- `defer`: required evidence, consent, or authority is incomplete.
- `escalate`: a higher or different legitimate authority must decide.
- `refuse`: the action violates a hard constraint or lacks a lawful/ethical basis.

## Prohibition

No Compass result, aggregate benefit claim, or AI recommendation may override valid refusal, protected rights, collective/community authority, or an applicable safety boundary.
